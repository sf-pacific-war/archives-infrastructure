Program Overview

SF Pacific War Archives Infrastructure Project

Overview of the program scope, core tracks, architectural lens, and institutional principles.

POC: JG Updated: 8/9/2026
Scope

Program scope

The program spans preservation, collections, interoperability, and public discovery.

  • Collection inventory, digitization, and preservation readiness
  • Collections metadata, archival description, and governance
  • External archival interoperability and cross-institution discovery
  • Discovery, retrieval, and AI-assisted access through a digital reading room

Together these areas define the program boundary.

Tracks

Four program tracks

Four tracks define the program.

Track 1 — Collection Inventory & Digitization

Collection inventory and digitization

Identification, inventory, prioritization, and digitization of local collection materials and born-digital assets.

  • What the institution actually holds
  • What has already been digitized
  • What is still analog or poorly organized
  • What can be publicly shown and what cannot

Track 2 — Digital Collections Management & Archival Description

Digital collections management and archival description

Structured management and description of local digital records, including metadata, provenance, rights, review state, and authority control.

  • Record description rules
  • Required fields
  • Separation of source text and AI-derived text
  • Rights and review-state tracking

Track 3 — External Archives Metadata Aggregation & Interoperability

External archives metadata aggregation and interoperability

Metadata aggregation and mapping for materials held by outside institutions such as NARA and university archives.

  • Local representation of partner-held records
  • Source identifiers and provenance preservation
  • Metadata mapping into a canonical local profile
  • Link-out, ingest, and copy boundaries

Track 4 — Digital Reading Room: Discovery & Access

Digital reading room: discovery and access

User-facing discovery and access for approved records through search, filtering, structured detail views, and AI-assisted retrieval support.

  • Public discovery and research access
  • Record detail views and multilingual presentation
  • Search, filtering, and retrieval
  • Clear distinction between source truth and derived layers
Architecture Lens

Four connected architectural concerns

Preservation, collections, interoperability, and discovery describe the same program from connected technical layers.

Preservation

Files, fixity, storage evidence, preservation metadata, and durable stewardship.

Collections

Canonical metadata, hierarchy, provenance, rights, review state, and governed descriptive truth.

External archival interoperability

Mappings, identifiers, APIs, partner-held records, and cross-institution discovery.

Discovery & AI

Search, multilingual access, hybrid retrieval, and AI-assisted enrichment built on the layers below.

Preservation and collections carry source truth; interoperability connects archives; discovery and AI extend access without replacing source truth.

Principles

Institutional principles

These principles define the relationship between records, access, and AI.

Canonical record

The canonical record remains the source of truth.

Governance

Provenance, rights, and review state must remain explicit.

Derived layers

Discovery and AI outputs must remain derived, replaceable, and distinguishable from source truth.

AI integrity

LLMs may interpret or enrich records, but must not rewrite original records.

Public access rests on structured records, and cross-archive retrieval remains a managed interoperability layer.
